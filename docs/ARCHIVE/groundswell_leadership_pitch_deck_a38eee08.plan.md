# Groundswell Leadership Pitch Deck Plan

## What You Have

**TalentHud deck (8 slides)** — Clear product-pitch arc:

- Hero / tagline → **Problem** (flying blind; noise, lock-in, slow decisions) → **Solution** (AI layer, platform-agnostic) → **Two Modes** (Explorer vs Match) → **How It Works** (4 steps) → **Why It Matters** (stats + quote) → **Who It's For** (personas) → **CTA** (waitlist).

**Groundswell doc** — Initiative definition:

- **Why:** Large enterprises often have no MongoDB footprint not because of irrelevance, but overload, inherited decisions, and no safe way to evaluate. Sales-led motion is either too late or creates resistance.
- **Audience:** Senior architects, engineering managers, developers, AI Factories / Black Belts. Success = no extra work or friction for them.
- **Principles:** Trust before transactions; understanding before adoption; community before pipeline; explicit permission to disengage.
- **What it is / isn’t:** Engineer-first, low-commitment, optional, learning. Not a sales campaign, migration program, or roadmap change.
- **Risk addressed:** Discovering limitations too late, after lock-in; goal = earlier, lower-stakes exploration.
- **Motion:** Presence → Enablement → Community → Opportunity (sequential, don’t skip).
- **Activities:** Bi-weekly sessions, DevRel setup, problem-driven discussions, hackathon-lite, product updates; voluntary; if engagement drops, stop.
- **Opportunities:** Emerge when engineers ask “Could this work here? At our scale? Cost?” — then bring in sales.
- **Internal use:** Repeatable model; pilot in a few accounts, 60–90 days, then decide on deeper investment.
- **Feedback:** Strategic bets, narratives (monolith, AI), pain points, speaking at customer events, inject into their cadence, strong coach/sponsor, “known face” and network, build demos with SAs (e.g. NorBenLux), brainstorm topics, Christian Uloth session.

## Should you demo the tool? What would bedazzle?

**Recommendation: Yes — keep it short and live.** A 2–3 minute demo right after the "Delivery & enablement" slide (slide 8) shows that Groundswell has a real, working asset, not just slides.

**What would make the team sit up:**

- **Interactive, not slideware:** Show the workshop app running — pick one lab (e.g. encryption or query), run a step, show the verification / progress. Emphasise that customers get hands-on learning, not another deck.
- **Gamification:** If you have a leaderboard or progress tracking, show it. "People compete, stay engaged, and remember the session" is a strong message for Sales and SA.
- **Fun and memorable:** One line to say: "This is the kind of engagement that sticks — fun, interactive, and something they can use the same day."
- **Team effort, scale-out:** Right after the demo, reinforce: "This is one way we deliver. DevRel and SAs can use this plus other collateral — demos, docs, hackathons. The point is we build and run together and scale out."

**Risks to avoid:** Don't let the demo run long (2–3 min max). Have a fallback (short screen recording or 2–3 static screens) in case of connectivity or setup issues.

## Content and Messaging Notes

- **Tone:** Match the doc — conservative, trust-first, “no disruption to delivery.” Avoid hype; speak to skeptical senior architects and busy engineering managers.
- **Differentiate from “MongoDB Day”:** Call out explicitly: this is about building a sustained network and being a known, trusted “face,” not a one-off event.
- **SA role:** Emphasize building demos and content collaboratively with SAs (feedback: NorBenLux, brainstorm) so SA leadership sees clear ownership.
- **Sales handoff:** Make the trigger clear: we involve sales when engineers start asking fit/scale/cost questions — so Sales sees how pipeline is created without cold pushing.
- **Success metrics:** The doc marks “How do you measure success? TBD.” For tomorrow, suggest 2–3 placeholder metrics (e.g. engagement level, number of “pull” requests, pipeline sourced from Groundswell) and label as “proposed — to refine with you.”
